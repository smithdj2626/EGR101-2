Standard 15-Week Design Project Timeline

Calibrated for 1 to 2 hours per student per week (approx. 15–30 total student-hours).

Phase Benchmarks
Weeks 1-3 Phase 1

Problem & Scope Definition

  • Identify target user or process pain point
  • Formulate measurable target metrics
  • Review existing solutions & prior art
Budget: ~3-4 hrs total
Weeks 4-6 Phase 2

Concept Generation & Matrix

  • Brainstorm 3 distinct design concepts
  • Perform Pugh Decision Matrix evaluation
  • Create low-fidelity paper/CAD sketch
Budget: ~4-5 hrs total
Weeks 7-9 Phase 3

Initial Prototype Assembly

  • Procure low-cost materials / 3D prints
  • Build Version 1.0 (physical rig or flowchart)
  • Safety & hazard self-assessment
Budget: ~5-6 hrs total
Weeks 10-12 Phase 4

Quantitative Testing & Refinement

  • Run 3+ controlled test trials
  • Collect data (time, load, voltage, flow)
  • Iterate prototype design based on data
Budget: ~5-6 hrs total
Weeks 13-15 Phase 5

Final Deliverable & Showcase

  • Compile engineering notebook data
  • Prepare poster or 5-min slide deck
  • Final prototype demonstration
Budget: ~4-5 hrs total

Instructor Scope Guardrails for 1-2 Hours/Week

Encourage Fast Iteration Prefer cardboard, foam board, hot glue, or Micro:bits over custom PCB design or complex metal machining which lead to bottlenecks.
Focus on Key Testing Metrics Require students to measure at least ONE quantitative metric (e.g. "reduced time by 22%", "carried 500g load", "filtered 90% turbidity").
Process vs. Product For process redesign projects (e.g., dining hall or campus parking workflow), student prototypes can consist of physical mockups, simulated queues, or detailed flow diagrams.
